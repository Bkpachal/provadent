Supplement labels pack a lot of information into a small space. Knowing how to read one properly makes it much easier to compare products honestly — including ProvaDent.

Serving Size vs. Servings Per Container

The serving size tells you how much to take at once (1 capsule for ProvaDent); servings per container tells you how long one bottle actually lasts (60 capsules per bottle). Multiply the two together before assuming a bottle will last a full month.

Named Amounts vs. Proprietary Blends

Some labels list the exact milligram amount next to every ingredient; others group several ingredients into one "blend" with a single combined total. On ProvaDent's own label, each ingredient amount is listed individually on the label. Neither approach alone tells you whether a product works, but named amounts make it easier to compare against published research.

"Other Ingredients"

Below the main ingredient panel, most labels list "other ingredients" — things like capsule shells, binders or preservatives. These are not the active ingredients but are worth a glance if you have known sensitivities.

Reading ProvaDent's Label Specifically

ProvaDent lists 8 named ingredients. Probiotic Blend for Oral Flora is included for its role in supports a balanced mouth microbiome. Calcium is included for its role in structural mineral for teeth. Vitamin D3 is included for its role in helps the body absorb and use calcium. Vitamin C is included for its role in antioxidant support for gum tissue.

Cost Per Serving, Not Just Cost Per Bottle

A bigger bottle is not automatically a better deal. Divide the total price by the number of servings per bottle to get a true cost-per-day figure, then compare that across package sizes before deciding which size of ProvaDent to order.

Quick Checklist

  • Check the serving size, not just the bottle size.
  • Look for named amounts where possible.
  • Scan "other ingredients" for anything you need to avoid.
  • Compare cost per serving, not just cost per bottle.
